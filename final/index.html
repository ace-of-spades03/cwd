Features of North American Dialects in the Continental United States

Even though we all speak English, that does not mean that all English is the same. English is made up of different dialects which are different ways of talking one language. Different dialects may pronounce words differently or have different words to refer to the same thing.


Now, this representation of dialectology, the study of dialects, is not meant to serve as a strict definition of how each U.S. states talks. It is a general representation of each state's majority dialectal features. Dialectology is a complex field, and this representation seeks to bring people into the beautiful world that is American Dialects. However, these representations will never be perfect because dialects are:

  1. Always changing
  2. Highly related to social category (race, gender, social class, and more)
  3. Primarily researched in old, White, rural men

Regardless of the flaws, I hope that this representation will interest you, and that it will inspire you to think carefully before you correct people based on what you believe is "correct English." There is no truly correct English, and there are so many interesting things that come from differences in English!

While dialects can vary in a state based on county, race, gender, class, and whether the person is a native to the state, there are general trends in U.S. dialects. Generally, states fall into the West, the North, the Northern Central, the Midlands, the South, the Inland North, or the Northern New England dialects. This is determined based on lexicon (word choices), accent (vocal quality of speaking), and syntax (sentence contsruction).

Cot-Caught Merger

One feature of English that is currently changing is the pronunciation of the words "cot" and "caught." Some dialects have the merger, which means that the words are pronounced the same, but other dialects keep the pronunciations different.

Lightning Bugs or Fireflies

Different dialects refer to this creature as different things. This creature is the small bug with a behind that lights up. The most popular ways to refer to it are "lightning bug" and "firefly."

You, You Guys, or Y'all

Different dialects address multiple people in the second person in different ways. It may include "y'all," "you all," "you guys," "yins," etc.

Different dialects refer to carbonated drinks in different ways. These words include "soda," "pop," "soft drink," etc.

A feature of dialects that may cause strife between you and your friends is the pronunciation of words. A word that is commonly pronounced differently by different dialects is "caramel." The primary difference is the syllable count, either two or three syllables, but there are other pronunciations that exist.

Crawfish, Crayfish, or Crawdad

Different dialects refer to the small lobster-like animal in rivers differently. It may be a "crawfish," a "crayfish," or even a "crawdad."

Crayon

Another word that is pronounced differently by different dialects is "crayon." The different common pronunciations are very distinct in syllables and vowels; it can be pronounced "cran," "cray-on," or even "crown."
